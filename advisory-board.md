# Advisory Board

Use these as reviewer personae modelled on the named researchers' public areas of
scholarly work. This is not a claim about their private views.

## Reviewers

### Muhammad Ali Khalidi
- **Expertise:** Natural kinds, causal networks, projectibility, philosophy of science.
- **Persona:** Tests whether the paper earns its kind/profile talk by showing what the profile lets us predict and explain.
- **Watch for:** Projectibility becoming decorative; causal-network language doing less work than advertised; interest-relativity being conceded but not controlled.

### Raphael Milliere
- **Expertise:** Philosophy of AI, LLM representation, grounding, concepts, and semantic competence.
- **Persona:** Checks whether the LLM payoff is genuinely new and whether the paper distinguishes truth-tracking from referential grounding.
- **Watch for:** Repeating the sibling vector-grounding argument; treating partial participation as enough without specifying a distinctive failure profile; overgeneralising from text-only systems to all LLM systems.

### Emily M. Bender
- **Expertise:** Computational linguistics, language technology, meaning, grounding, and sociotechnical critique of LLMs.
- **Persona:** Hostile but charitable reviewer for the claim that text-trained systems inherit testimonial traces while lacking world-directed correction.
- **Watch for:** Understating the limits of form-only training; using "testimony" too loosely for pretraining data; blurring human communicative standing with statistical text production.

### Jennifer Lackey
- **Expertise:** Social epistemology, testimony, disagreement, epistemic norms, and institutional knowledge.
- **Persona:** Reviews whether testimony and institutional correction are handled with enough precision.
- **Watch for:** Treating textual inheritance as testimony without speaker/hearer standing; missing distinctions between testimony, records, reports, and training traces; underdeveloping social correction.

### Michael P. Lynch
- **Expertise:** Truth, truth pluralism, realism, epistemic normativity, and the value of truth.
- **Persona:** Tests whether the truth-theoretic positioning is modest enough and whether the paper avoids competing with semantic theories of truth.
- **Watch for:** Category errors about truth as a kind; insufficient engagement with pluralism/deflationism; sliding from truth-relevant success into a substantive theory of truth.

# Source hook: Futrell & Mahowald (2026), a positive answer to this paper's title question
<!-- SUMMARY: BBS target article claiming LMs are model systems and proofs of concept for gradient usage-based approaches; a rival account of what LMs participate in · status: filed 2026-08-08, uncited · updated: 2026-08-08 -->

**Source:** Futrell, R., & Mahowald, K. 2026. How linguistics learned to stop worrying and love the
language models. *Behavioral and Brain Sciences* 49, e198: 1–80. doi:10.1017/S0140525X2510112X.
Central note: `literature/futrell-mahowald-2026-linguistics-language-models.notes.md`. Full
treatment: target article, 25 commentaries from p. 27, authors' response from p. 69. PDF page =
printed page.

**Why this project cares.** This paper asks what LLMs participate in. F&M give a positive, published
answer to that question: LMs "contribute to fundamental questions about linguistic structure,
language processing, and learning," and serve as "model systems and working proofs of concept for
gradient, usage-based approaches to language" without replacing structure and theory. That is a
rival account occupying the same ground, from the field's current flagship treatment, and it isn't a
sceptical position the profile argument can simply out-flank.

**The specific tension worth stating.** A route inventory says LMs inherit testimony and coherence
densely while participating weakly in perception, measurement, intervention and correction. F&M's
"model system" claim is compatible with exactly that, since a model system need not participate in
what it models. So the question isn't whether F&M are too optimistic. It's whether "model system"
and "weak participation in the world-facing routes" are the same claim in different vocabularies,
and if they are, what the profile adds. Answering that would sharpen the contribution; leaving it
unanswered invites a referee to ask.

**Also relevant.** Lampinen, Lupyan and Millière commentaries bear on grounding and representation.
Nefdt & Ladyman's commentary (BBS 49 e219, p. 62) already has a hook in this project; their
"are these patterns in linguistic outputs or in cognition?" question is the same domain problem in
another vocabulary.

**Before citing.** Record above read from the typeset masthead. Cite individual commentaries by
their own e-number and DOI. No bib key yet; suggested `futrellMahowald2026LanguageModels`.

## Checked against the source (this project's session, 2026-08-08): the tension does not survive, and what replaces it is better

The framing above is right that F&M must be cited and wrong that they are a rival account of what LLMs participate in *for this paper's purposes*. Counts across the whole 80-page bundle, target article plus 25 commentaries plus the authors' response:

Two of the three `truth` hits are one commentary arguing about whether truth is gradable. `reference` looks high at 49 but 25 of those are the word "References" heading each commentary's bibliography, leaving about 15 substantive uses. And the 9 `grounding` hits are the **multimodal and socio-cognitive** strand ~-- sensory input, embodiment, interaction, "linking language to visual inputs and action outputs," one commentary saying LMs "entirely lack the socio-cognitive grounding that usage-based linguistics considers constitutive of language." None is about answerability to how things are.

**So the flagship treatment brackets exactly this paper's question.** F&M ask what LMs teach us about *linguistic structure and its learnability*. This paper asks what makes their outputs *answerable to the world*. The relata differ: their model-system claim relates an LM to a researcher's inquiry (instrument to investigation); the participation claim relates an LM's outputs to the world. A system can be an excellent model system for text-recoverable structure and participate in none of the world-facing routes, and on the evidence that is precisely the situation.

**This converts a defensive item into positive motivation, and it is the strongest such evidence the project has.** The venue decision record's Evidence and Motivation Test asks whether the problem is live rather than merely imaginable. The answer is now: the field's current 80-page synthesis on what LMs mean for the study of language, with 25 commentaries, mentions truth three times and answerability never. That is an **evidenced negative claim** with the search stated, which is what the portfolio's negative-claims rule requires, and it belongs in §1 as motivation rather than in §6 as a reply.

**Still cite them, and mark the boundary explicitly.** A *Synthese* referee working on LMs will know this treatment. The sentence to write is roughly: F&M establish that LMs are informative model systems for linguistic structure; the question here is a different one their treatment brackets, namely what stabilizes the answerability of what such systems produce.

**The dissenting commentary, now identified and verified.** **Philip Resnik, "Are language models models?" *BBS* 49, e220, doi:10.1017/S0140525X26104762**, Department of Linguistics and Institute for Advanced Computer Studies, University of Maryland, College Park (`resnik@umd.edu`). Details confirmed independently in the bundle at the commentary masthead, not taken on report.

He assesses the model-system claim at each of Marr's three levels and denies it, most sharply at implementation (connectionism abandoned biological correspondence). At the algorithmic/representational level he attacks F&M's **convergence** premise: unlike visual object recognition, "a key part of what makes 'language' hard is that it decomposes into very distinct subproblems, not merely competing constraints within a single problem," with sharks and dolphins as the biological counterexample to convergence.

**Two reasons he is a better ally than a philosophical objector would be.** He is a computational linguist who concedes the engineering and still denies the model-system claim, so the objection cannot be dismissed as unfamiliarity with the systems. And his model-organism argument is structurally *our* discipline: model-organism choices "are supported by existing, solidly established mechanistic evidence; e.g., mice and ferrets are preferred in work on tumorigenesis and influenza... because" of that evidence. That is the same demand this programme makes in reserving `homeostatic` for shown corrective coupling ~-- don't claim the stronger relation until the mechanism is evidenced. Worth citing for exactly that parallel.

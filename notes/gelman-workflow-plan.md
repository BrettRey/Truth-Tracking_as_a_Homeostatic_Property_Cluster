# Gelman-style model-checking extension

## Purpose

Add a reproducible fake-data exercise that asks whether the proposed decomposition does useful predictive and intervention-guiding work. The exercise will not claim to identify the decomposition against unrestricted effective coverage. Its point is asymmetric: a fixed decomposition may survive demanding checks, but it must also be capable of losing to a prespecified conditional-coverage model.

## Projective claim to test

From measurements of route identity, route relations, and the five corrective-control features, predict:

1. final correctness and harmful intervention on held-out route–task combinations;
2. intermediate failures of invocation, detection, attribution, and uptake;
3. which equal-cost intervention will produce the largest target-improving gain in a new arrangement.

The source information, population of arrangements and tasks, perturbations, loss function, and failure conditions will be fixed in the simulation script rather than adjusted after seeing the results.

## Models

1. **Input coverage.** Uses baseline task information available before the route under test produces a signal.
2. **Conditional coverage.** Uses a prespecified measure of the route's incremental information given the generator's answer, including conditional error overlap, but not a post-hoc redefinition after each intervention.
3. **Structural decomposition.** Uses route–task fit, conditional error overlap, attribution, and uptake to predict the stages of correction and the final output.
4. **Unrestricted effective coverage.** Appears only as the non-identifiability benchmark: because it may encode every successful mechanism after the fact, it is not fitted as an empirical rival.

## Fake-data scenarios

### A. Uptake matters beyond conditional information

Hold the checker signal and its conditional information fixed while changing whether the same signal is advisory or enforced. The decomposition should predict the uptake intervention and the conditional-coverage model should miss it. This tests whether the design can recover a case in which architecture adds something beyond evidence possession.

### B. Conditional coverage is sufficient

Hold attribution and uptake fixed and generate outcomes wholly from the route's incremental information. Conditional coverage should match the decomposition on held-out prediction and intervention choice. The decomposition then fails to earn explanatory surplus; additional parameters count against it.

### C. The declared features are measured poorly

Generate a route-structured process but add realistic measurement error to the feature estimates. If conditional coverage predicts held-out outcomes or intervention choices better, the practical decomposition fails even though its latent mechanism generated the data. This prevents an unmeasurable architecture from receiving credit merely for being true in the simulation.

## Workflow and checks

1. Simulate hierarchical item, task-family, and arrangement variation from known parameters.
2. Confirm parameter recovery and sensible predictions on fake data generated by each candidate structure.
3. Train the models on selected route–task combinations and test them on combinations not used for fitting.
4. Compare log predictive score and Brier score for final correctness, plus calibration plots.
5. Check intermediate observables separately: detection conditional on generator error, attribution, uptake, successful repair, and false intervention.
6. Give each model the same equal-cost intervention choice and report decision regret against the known data-generating process.
7. Repeat across seeds and parameter ranges rather than presenting one favourable run.

## Declared failure conditions

The decomposition fails the claimed projective role if any of the following persists across reasonable simulations:

- a fixed conditional-coverage model matches or improves its held-out prediction;
- conditional coverage ranks interventions just as well with fewer assumptions;
- the five features do not determine an intervention ranking without case-specific additions;
- the structural model predicts final accuracy while missing the intermediate correction process;
- feature measurement error makes the decomposition unusable at plausible sample sizes.

Failure to distinguish the decomposition from unrestricted effective coverage is not counted against it, because that equivalence follows by construction.

- Put the model specification, scenario table, diagnostics, and results in Online Resource 1.
- Add at most one compact paragraph to §5.7 of the article, stating what the fake-data exercise establishes and, especially, how the decomposition loses.
- Do not add a new main-text figure unless the simulation reveals a compact result that materially improves the argument.
- Rebuild and visually inspect both PDFs after the analysis and prose agree.

## Stopping rule

Stop if the exercise merely demonstrates that mandatory uptake mechanically changes output or if the result depends on defining conditional coverage implausibly weakly. In that event, retain only the clearer failure criterion in the prose and do not enlarge the supplement with a ceremonial simulation.

## Implementation outcome

The first run revealed that a correctly specified structural model could improve held-out repair prediction without improving intervention choice. That result was retained rather than tuned away as a fourth constructed world. The final workflow therefore distinguishes predictive surplus from practical decision surplus and includes three ways for the decomposition to fail.

A separate bounded Alloy audit was added as an internal logical check. Once the five features were derived from actual route structure rather than represented as free labels, Alloy still produced an arrangement in which every feature was present on some route but no single connected correction path existed. Section 4 now states the resulting joint-instantiation condition. The Alloy formalization remains outside the article and supplement.

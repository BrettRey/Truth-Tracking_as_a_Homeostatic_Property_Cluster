# Section 5 Expansion Plan

## Target

Section 5 has to make the profile view do work that the component theories do
not already do. The existing TODOs identify the right two tasks:

1. make co-variation carry epistemic weight in a worked conflict case;
2. characterize "jointly disciplined" independently enough to avoid circularity.

## Proposed Move

Expand Section 5 by making the unit of assessment a linked profile, not a single
source. The contrast with reliabilism should be fair: Goldman is right that
causal process reliability matters. The profile claim is narrower. In some
cases, the evidential fact is not just that perception, testimony, or inference
is generally reliable, but that their outputs have historically corrected and
calibrated one another in the relevant domain.

The worked case should be modest and generic: a visual judgment conflicts with
an instrument reading and with well-attested testimony. The profile view should
not say "always trust the instrument" or "always trust testimony." It should say
that the verdict depends on how the stabilisers have been coupled in that
domain. In everyday object recognition, perception, testimony, and practical
feedback have long corrected each other, so a lone odd testimony report carries
less weight. In a mature instrumental domain, perception is often the weak node,
because instruments, calibration routines, expert testimony, and repeatable
interventions form the tighter profile.

## Anti-Circularity Move

Define connectivity without first checking whether the mechanisms agree. The
three proposed proxies can be made clearer:

- shared selection or training history: have the mechanisms been shaped by the
  same recurring tasks and error signals?
- institutional age: has the practice had time to build correction norms,
  calibration routines, records, and role differentiation?
- measurement infrastructure: are there instruments, standards, replications,
  and intervention routines that allow one stabiliser to correct another?

Then the prediction is not "agreement is where they agree." It is: disagreement
should cluster where those independent coupling conditions are thin.

## Citations To Use Cautiously

- Goldman for belief-forming processes, reliability, and the generality problem.
- BonJour for coherence as system-level inferential connection and the need for
  truth-conduciveness.
- Lackey for testimony as requiring both speaker reliability and hearer-side
  epistemic contribution.
- Khalidi for causal relations and projectibility.
- Onishi and Serpico, with Craver, for interest-relativity of causal-network
  boundaries.

## What Not To Do

- Do not describe the profile as literally homeostatic.
- Do not make the example depend on a real empirical case that has not been
  sourced.
- Do not overstate that reliabilism cannot discuss process reliability. The
  difference is that reliabilism evaluates process types, while the profile view
  evaluates cross-source coupling and predicts where that coupling breaks down.
- Do not let Section 5 become a survey of component theories.

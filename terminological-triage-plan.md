# Terminological triage plan

## Decision

The review is right about the structural problem. The next revision should reduce the
reader's formal memory burden rather than add another crosswalk. The headline paper is the
grounding/corrective-control argument; the general profile theory should support that
argument without becoming a second coequal theory readers must master first.

The revision will retain one two-level architecture:

1. **Which routes constrain the arrangement, and how are they related?** Records,
   perception, measurement, intervention, coherence, checking, practical feedback, and
   their calibration or error relations determine what kind of constraint is available
   and where an intervention should help.
2. **Given those routes, how much corrective control does the arrangement have over this
   target?** Target access, detectability, checking-route independence, attribution, and
   effective uptake determine whether a fresh discrepancy can become independently visible
   and alter the output in a target-improving direction.

The profile identifies the routes and their relations. The five features diagnose
corrective control within that profile.

## Load-bearing assumptions and tests

| Assumption | What would falsify it? | Test before or during revision |
|---|---|---|
| The grounding/corrective-control argument does not require the four-position taxonomy. | A central prediction cannot be stated without assigning routes to the four named positions. | Trace every empirical prediction and rewrite it using route identity and coupling plus the five arrangement-level features. |
| The four participation modes can be replaced by the historical/live distinction. | A later argument relies on the modes as mutually exclusive explanatory values. | Audit every occurrence; preserve the causal fact in ordinary prose and retain only derivative versus live answerability as a local distinction. |
| The maintenance partial order is not load-bearing here. | Removing it makes the projective claim infer stability, maintenance, or control without qualification. | Replace it with one paragraph separating recurrence, causal support, maintenance, and correction; rerun the projectibility audit. |
| *Route* can do the ordinary work now assigned to *stabilizer*. | The Boyd/Khalidi argument needs *stabilizer* as a distinct ontological category. | Retain one initial gloss, “stabilizing routes,” and preserve the historical comparison with Millikan; use *route* thereafter. |
| The introductory schematic can be removed without losing orientation. | The revised introduction cannot state bearer, profile, answerability, corrective control, and empirical payoff in one readable paragraph. | Replace the figure with the four-term verbal spine below and read the introduction/conclusion as a pair. |

## Vocabulary after triage

### Core terms used throughout

- **arrangement** — the language model plus whatever records, tools, sensors, checkers, or
  reviewers are live for the target and task;
- **profile** — which routes the arrangement has and how they interact;
- **answerability** — the graded relation in which discrepancies can affect an output;
- **corrective control** — the stronger arrangement-level capacity for live detection and
  repair.

### Diagnostic terms introduced together in the corrective-control section

- target access;
- detectability;
- checking-route independence;
- attribution;
- effective uptake.

### Local theoretical terms

- **projectibility** — introduced with Goodman and Khalidi when the paper turns to bounded
  expectations about nearby cases;
- **calibration history** — introduced only where it helps predict current error relations
  or stability under perturbation;
- derivative and live answerability;
- the three readings of *coverage*, confined to the coverage passage.

### Local distinctions to retain without promoting them into a second taxonomy

- causal ancestry, derivative answerability, and live answerability;
- inherited structure through training versus a route live in the current arrangement;
- the three readings of *coverage*, confined to the coverage objection.

### Terms to demote or remove

- use **route** normally; introduce **stabilizing route** once and reserve *stabilizing
  function* for the Millikan/Grindrod comparison;
- remove **bearer** as a marked term, while stating once that the arrangement bears the
  profile;
- use *participates* as an ordinary causal verb and remove **Participation** as a marked
  noun;
- remove the four named participation modes;
- remove the four named architectural positions and Table 1;
- remove the partial-order schematic and demote **stability**, **directed dependence**, and
  **effective maintenance** to one ordinary-language qualification;
- replace **realized corrective effectiveness** with **conditional repair performance**;
- remove **documentary inheritance** as a named relation;
- present the three coverage readings locally without treating them as permanent
  architectural terms.

## Structural edits

### 1. Introduction: replace the ontology with a verbal spine

Remove the introductory schematic and its dedicated TikZ styles. Delay *projectibility*
until Goodman and Khalidi enter later. Replace the current sequence of definitions with a
paragraph along these lines:

> An arrangement is a language model together with whatever records, tools, sensors,
> checkers, or reviewers are live for a particular target and task. Its profile records the
> routes through which target information and correction can affect its outputs. An output
> is answerable to a target to the extent that discrepancies can affect what the arrangement
> produces or accepts; it falls under corrective control when live routes can detect and
> repair those discrepancies. The profile earns its keep if it predicts where interventions
> will and won't transfer.

Retain the three-contribution sequence, but express the third contribution as selective
transfer and held-out transport rather than introducing *projection* and *projectibility*
before the reader needs them.

### 2. Sections 2–4: make route identity and coupling plus five features the sole decomposition

- Keep the target and answerability examples in Section 2.
- Rename Section 3 from “Truth-tracking as a stabilizer profile” to “Routes and profiles.”
- Replace the four-position taxonomy and Table 1 with a short paragraph: some routes bring
  target information in, some integrate it, some expose discrepancies, and some return
  practical failure to production. These are jobs routes can combine, not positions readers
  must memorize.
- Preserve Goodman, Khalidi, Boyd, the fever example, and the distinction between a stable
  pattern and live correction, but make each serve the transfer claim directly.
- Rename Section 4 to foreground projectible predictions and corrective control.
- Remove the maintenance partial order and its schematic. Preserve only the proposition
  that a profile can support bounded projection without active correction, while corrective
  control is one way stability may be maintained.
- Introduce the five features as the paper's only detailed diagnostic architecture.
- Keep the capacity/performance distinction but call the empirical quantity *conditional
  repair performance*.

### 3. Section 5 and objections: preserve arguments, remove obsolete scaffolding

- Retain calibration history and the component-theory comparisons.
- Rewrite references to “stabilizers,” “positions,” “participation modes,” and maintenance
  levels in ordinary route language.
- Keep the preservation condition for inherited textual support, but remove *documentary
  inheritance* as an additional term.
- Preserve interest-relativity, fictionalism, non-empirical domains, and the failure
  conditions for projectibility.

### 4. Section 7: organize around inherited structure and live routes

- Rename the section “The LLM case: inherited structure and live routes.”
- Rename §7.1 “The arrangement and its routes.”
- Replace the four participation modes with two questions: what constraint arrived through
  training, and what route is live now? Keep causal ancestry distinct from derivative
  answerability: training influence carries epistemic support only when it preserves the
  effects of target-sensitive correction.
- Retain Figure 1 because it gives one memorable contrast: derivative versus live
  answerability.
- Rewrite later occurrences of *operational*, *corrective*, and *decorative participation*
  as concrete claims about live information, correction, or merely apparent tool use.
- Keep the route-specific intervention table, the checker-independence design, the temporal
  design, and the coverage identification limit.

### 5. Coverage and conclusion

- Present input, conditional, and effective coverage as three readings introduced for one
  objection, not as additions to the global architecture.
- Keep the reduced-form/structural distinction and the fixed-application-condition burden.
- Rewrite the conclusion so it asks readers to remember only arrangement, profile,
  answerability, corrective control, the five features, and selective transfer.

## Preservation constraints

The revision must not weaken or remove:

- the arrangement as the target- and task-indexed bearer;
- the distinction between inherited historical influence and a live correction route;
- the five corrective-control features;
- the distinction between corrective capacity and conditional repair performance;
- the coverage identification limit;
- prespecification, held-out transport, intervention choice, and declared failure
  conditions;
- the claim that route-by-task interactions are not unique to the profile;
- the paper's existing evidence, citations, estimands, thresholds, and qualifications.

## Verification and stopping rule

After the rewrite:

1. inventory all marked terms and confirm that no removed taxonomy survives in headings,
   captions, tables, cross-references, or conclusion;
2. ensure each empirical prediction can be derived from route identity and coupling plus
   the five arrangement-level corrective features;
3. rerun the projectibility audit, bibliography validation, and house-style checks;
4. rebuild and visually inspect every page, especially the former Table 1 and introductory
   figure locations;
5. read the abstract, introduction, Section 7 opening, and conclusion as a single spine;
6. apply a reader-memory test after each major section: it should be summarizable using the
   four core terms, route names, and—after the corrective-control section—the five
   diagnostic features;
7. stop if the rewrite begins inventing a replacement taxonomy or weakens a declared
   failure condition.

Baseline before triage: 35 PDF pages and 15,906 whitespace-delimited source words. The
source currently contains 54 instances of *stabilizer* and 32 of *participation*, so this
is a manuscript-wide revision rather than a search-and-replace.
